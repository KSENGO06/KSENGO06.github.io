LifeWord

An English vocabulary app for Ukrainian speakers that teaches words through real scenes instead of flat lists. Designed and built entirely by me, from the first idea to the design and the code.

Why I built it

I'm from Ukraine, and learning foreign languages is my hobby, so I know first-hand what makes a new language hard for a beginner. I couldn't find a really good app on the Ukrainian market for learning words and actually practising them, so I built one based on my own experience.

How it works

  1. See it. Each topic is a realistic scene with labelled objects, so words stick through visual memory.
  2. Hear it. Tapping a label plays the pronunciation, adding auditory memory.
  3. Study it. Every word has its own card with transcription, audio and translation.
  4. Practise it. Three levels of exercises lock the words in: translation, matching, listening and sentence building.

What I built

  • 50+ categories of words and phrasal verbs, each with images and audio
  • Progress tracking, daily goals and visit streaks
  • Switching pronunciation between an American and a British accent
  • Lives, hints and slow audio playback in exercises
  • Home screen widgets and reminder notifications
  • Profile, personal dictionary and LifeWord Plus subscription screens; sign-in with Firebase Auth
  • JSON-driven content model: adding a new category needs no code changes
  • A debug-only in-app tool for placing word labels on scene photos

Working with AI

I created the visuals, exercise content and voice-over together with AI tools. Writing precise prompts turned work that would take days into hours. Every image and audio file was still reviewed by hand. To save even more time, I wrote Python scripts that run from the terminal and turn whole word lists into audio through the ElevenLabs API.

Tablio

An iPad POS system for restaurants and cafés across Europe. My first commercial role: as the only iOS developer, I owned the whole iOS app and built it from scratch to a finished product.

Team

A small cross-functional team: me on iOS, an Android developer, a backend developer and a designer. Tasks came through Notion, and I implemented the app from the designer's Figma files.

I had run my own food business in Ukraine and used POS systems every day, so I often suggested better solutions for real restaurant workflows. The designer and I shaped many screens together: I explained what works best technically and for staff, and she turned it into the design.

What I built

  • Staff sign-in with a password
  • Order flow from creation to payment and closing, with order history
  • Floor plan: arranging tables by zone, table statuses and per-guest orders
  • Payments in cash or by card, printed or electronic receipts, split bill
  • Menu management: products with variations, modifiers, ingredients and allergens
  • Staff management with roles, warehouse and stock, VAT settings
  • Role-based dashboards with separate widgets for owners, managers and waiters

Technical decisions

The backend wasn't ready when I started, so I built the app mock-data-first on Clean Architecture. Every data source sits behind a protocol in a repository layer, so the real API can replace the mock data without changing the UI or business logic. This let the iOS app move forward in parallel with the backend instead of waiting for it.

Hardest challenge

The floor plan: adding and arranging tables, and carrying a table's order from the floor plan into the checkout on the main menu screen, so staff could keep adding items and close the bill without losing anything along the way.
